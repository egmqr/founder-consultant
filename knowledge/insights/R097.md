title: Delete DNS records when the service behind them is gone
areas: [SEC]
checks: [SEC-23]
---
**Summary.** When a staging site, demo or campaign page on a hosting platform is deleted, its DNS record often stays behind, still pointing at the platform. Anyone can then claim that slot on the platform and serve phishing pages or malware under your subdomain, and users trust it because the address bar shows your name. A taken-over subdomain also sits inside your domain's trust: cookies scoped to the parent domain and loose redirect allowlists reach it.

**Takeaways**
- Inventory every DNS record that points at a third-party platform and flag those whose target no longer exists
- When a resource is removed, its record is removed the same day, not parked
- Session cookies are scoped to the exact host that needs them, not the whole parent domain
- Auth redirect allowlists name exact hosts, never a wildcard subdomain
- A scheduled scan alerts when a new dangling record appears
